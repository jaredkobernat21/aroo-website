Ticks in Kansas

Common Kansas ticks, tick season, and how to protect your dog.

Updated November 2026 · By aroo, a locally owned Topeka dog poop cleanup service

Ticks are a real concern for dogs in eastern Kansas, including Topeka. Here’s what to know, based on guidance from Kansas State University and the Kansas Department of Health and Environment (KDHE).

Common ticks in Kansas

  • Lone star tick. Now the most abundant tick in Kansas, according to K-State. It can carry the bacteria that cause ehrlichiosis in dogs and people.
  • American dog tick. Long one of the most commonly reported ticks in the state. It can transmit Rocky Mountain spotted fever to dogs and people, and can cause tick paralysis in dogs.
  • Blacklegged (deer) tick. The tick that carries Lyme disease. K-State describes it as relatively uncommon in Kansas.
  • Brown dog tick. Mainly infests dogs.

When is tick season?

KDHE says ticks in Kansas are active from about March through September, and early warm spells can bring them out sooner. Activity peaks in the warm months.

How to check your dog

Ticks don’t jump or fly. They wait on grass and brush and grab on as your dog walks past. After walks in tall grass or wooded areas, K-State recommends checking:

  • In and around the ears and on the head
  • Under the legs
  • Around the tail

How to remove a tick

  1. Grab it close to the skin with tweezers or your fingers.
  2. Pull straight out with steady pressure. Don’t twist, burn, or smother it, which can make things worse.
  3. Save it in a sealed bag in the freezer in case your vet wants to identify it.
  4. Watch your dog over the next few weeks and call your vet about fever, low energy, limping, or loss of appetite.

Prevention

  • Use a vet-recommended tick preventive for your dog. KDHE warns against using human insect repellents on pets, and K-State warns that garlic-based “natural” products are toxic to dogs and cats.
  • Keep grass short and trim brush along fence lines, where ticks wait.
  • Check after every walk in tall grass, parks, and trails.
