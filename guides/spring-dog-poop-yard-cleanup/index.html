How to clean up a yard full of dog poop

A step-by-step spring thaw guide for Kansas yards.

Updated November 2026 · By aroo, a locally owned Topeka dog poop cleanup service

Every Kansas winter, dog poop freezes and gets buried under snow, then shows up all at once when it melts. Here’s how to get a yard full of dog poop back to clean.

When to do it

Start as soon as the snow melts and the ground is soft enough to work. The longer waste sits in spring rain, the more it breaks apart and soaks into the lawn. Waiting until the grass is tall makes piles harder to find.

What you need

  • A pooper scooper with a rake or spade (a shovel helps for heavy buildup)
  • Sturdy trash bags, or a bucket lined with a bag
  • Disposable or washable gloves
  • Shoes you can hose off

Step by step

  1. Work in a grid. Walk the yard in rows, like mowing, so you don’t miss spots. Start with the areas your dog uses most.
  2. Check the hiding spots. Fence lines, corners, under decks and bushes, and along the side of the house.
  3. Bag as you go. Don’t overfill bags. Tie them tightly and put them in your trash bin with the lid closed.
  4. Make a second pass. After a day or two of melting or rain, more turns up. A quick second walk catches what was buried.
  5. Clean up yourself and your tools. Hose off the scooper and your shoes, and wash your hands well.

Common questions

Can I put dog poop in compost or yard waste?

No. Dog poop can carry parasites like roundworm, so keep it out of compost and yard-waste bins. Bag it and put it in the trash.

Should I mow over it to break it up?

No. Mowing spreads waste across the lawn and onto your mower, and the parasite eggs stay in the grass.

How do I keep it from happening next winter?

Keep picking up through the winter whenever you can, even if it’s just near the door. Weekly service year-round means there’s nothing waiting for you in spring.
