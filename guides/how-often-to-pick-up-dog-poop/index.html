How often should you pick up dog poop?

How often to clean up your yard, why it matters, and how to make it easier.

Updated November 2026 · By aroo, a locally owned Topeka dog poop cleanup service

The short answer

Pick up dog poop in your yard at least once a week, and more often if you can. The CDC recommends disposing of dog poop promptly. Daily is ideal if kids play in the yard or you have more than one dog. Weekly is the minimum for keeping your yard clean, safe, and smell-free.

Why weekly matters

Parasites can outlast the poop

Roundworm eggs passed in dog poop take 2 to 4 weeks in the environment to become infectious, and their tough outer shell lets them survive in soil for months or even years, according to the CDC. Picking up within days removes eggs before they spread into your soil, where kids play and dogs sniff.

Rain carries it into our water

Pet waste left on the ground can be washed by rain into storm drains, which often flow straight to streams and lakes without treatment. EPA and stormwater fact sheets describe pet waste as a source of bacteria and of nutrients that feed algae.

It isn’t fertilizer

Dog poop is high in nitrogen and can burn grass, leaving brown and yellow patches. It doesn’t break down quickly enough to help your lawn.

Smell and flies

The longer poop sits, especially after rain or in summer heat, the more it smells and the more flies it attracts.

How often, by situation

One dog, small yard

Every few days, and at least weekly.

Two or more dogs

Every day or two. Waste builds up fast.

Kids play in the yard

Daily, plus a quick check before playtime.

After winter

A full spring cleanup as soon as the snow melts.

How to make it easier

  • Pick a set day. The same day each week is easier to remember.
  • Walk the yard in a grid so you don’t miss spots along fence lines and corners.
  • Bag it and put it in the trash with the lid closed. Don’t put it in compost or yard-waste bins.
  • Wash your hands afterward, even if you used a scooper or gloves.
  • Keep up with your dog’s deworming as your vet recommends.
  • Hand it off. A weekly pooper scooper service takes it off your list.

Common questions

Can I just mow over it?

No. Mowing spreads it across the lawn and onto your shoes and mower, and the parasite eggs stay in the grass.

What about in winter?

Poop freezes and gets buried in snow, then all appears at once in the spring thaw. Keeping up through winter makes spring much easier. If it’s already piled up, a one-time cleanup gets you back to zero.

Do I need to pick up in my own fenced yard?

Yes. The health and lawn reasons above apply in your own yard, not just in public.
